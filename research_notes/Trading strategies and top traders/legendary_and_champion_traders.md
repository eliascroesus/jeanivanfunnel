> **Verification grades used below:**
> - **A**: official competition record or regulator/audit-backed.
> - **B**: third-party journalistic or book account (Schwager's Market Wizards interviews, major press, Wikipedia citing press). These are not audits.
> - **C**: self-reported or marketing material from the trader or their firm.
> - **D**: anecdotal or single weak source (listicles, broker blogs).

## 1. Macro/discretionary legends (FX, index futures, gold): who they are, documented records, methods and risk rules

### Takeaway
The macro legends (Paul Tudor Jones, Soros/Druckenmiller, Kovner, Marcus, Lipschutz, Krieger) made their reputations on concentrated, leveraged, fundamentally driven bets in FX, bonds and stock indices. They timed these bets with technical tools such as Jones's 200-day moving average and his 1929 analog. Their defining risk traits are asymmetric reward:risk (Jones: 5:1), sizing to risk rather than to notional (Kovner), and cutting quickly when wrong. Nearly all headline return figures are grade B–D. None is publicly audited, and several conflict between sources.

### Cited Findings

#### Paul Tudor Jones (Tudor Investment Corp.): macro; stock-index, bond, FX and commodity futures
- **1987 record: sources conflict.** Wikipedia says his fund returned **125.9% after fees in 1987, earning an estimated $100M**. Other accounts citing Schwager's *Market Wizards* give **~62%** for the Tudor Futures Fund in October 1987, or about 62% for the year. The search found no source for the popular "tripled his money" claim. — [Wikipedia: Paul Tudor Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Tudor_Jones); contrasted with [Habrador blog](https://blog.habrador.com/2012/09/how-could-paul-tudor-jones-predict.html) and [Medium](https://medium.com/@vikaskhichi/paul-tudor-jones-and-the-art-of-aggressive-trading-03c70da7aa57) *(attribution approximate)*. **Grade B/C, with figures that conflict.**
- **The 1987 crash call:** his fund was heavily short the stock market going into Black Monday (Oct 19, 1987). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Tudor_Jones)
- **Historical analog method:** Tudor colleague Peter Borish anticipated the crash by mapping the 1987 market against the market before the 1929 crash. One stylized account claims a correlation above 92% across about 1,000 data points. That figure comes from a narrative piece and is unverified. — [The Felder Report](https://thefelderreport.com/2014/03/13/for-everyone-who-thinks-tom-demarks-1929-analog-is-a-joke); [Habrador](https://blog.habrador.com/2012/09/how-could-paul-tudor-jones-predict.html)
- **5:1 reward:risk rule, in his own words (Tony Robbins interview):** risk $1 to make $5. With that ratio a hit rate of only 20% is enough to come out ahead: "I could be wrong 80% of the time, and I'm still not going to lose." — [25iq](https://25iq.com/2015/07/25/a-dozen-things-ive-learned-from-paul-tudor-jones-about-investing-and-trading); [LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/blog/paul-tudor-jones-macro-playbook-for-traders/)
- **200-day moving average rule:** Jones called the 200-day average of closing prices his "metric for everything I look at". On the exit side: "if you use the 200-day moving average rule, then you get out. You play defense, and you get out." — [25iq](https://25iq.com/2015/07/25/a-dozen-things-ive-learned-from-paul-tudor-jones-about-investing-and-trading); [Antoine Buteau](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-paul-tudor-jones/)
- **Weakly sourced claims:**
  - Education blogs say he limits risk to **1% per trade** and that his 1987 short was triggered by the market falling below the 200-DMA.
  - A blog citing a Bloomberg snippet says the 200-DMA rule would have cut average drawdowns across six historic crashes from ~35% to ~6%.
  - Treat both as **unverified (grade D)**. — [LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/blog/paul-tudor-jones-iconic-trading-methods/); [trendfollowing.com](https://www.trendfollowing.com/paul-tudor-jones/)

#### Stanley Druckenmiller (Duquesne; Soros Quantum Fund 1988–2000) and George Soros: macro; FX, bonds, equity indices
- **Track record:** sources describe an average annual return of **about 30% with no losing year** over roughly 30 years at Duquesne. These figures are self-reported, and the search found no audit. **Grade B/C.** — [Wikipedia: Stanley Druckenmiller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Druckenmiller); [Hustle Fund](https://www.hustlefund.vc/post/angel-squad-stanley-druckenmiller-investments-the-macro-master-who-generated-30-annual-returns-without-a-single-down-year); [elEconomista](https://www.eleconomista.es/mercados-cotizaciones/amp/2385804/Duquesnes-Druckenmiller-retiring-after-30-years)
- **2010 closure:** the funds were reportedly down ~5% when he announced his retirement in August 2010, then erased the loss and closed with a small gain. Sources conflict on assets at closure: **$12B vs $23B**. — [elEconomista](https://www.eleconomista.es/mercados-cotizaciones/amp/2385804/Duquesnes-Druckenmiller-retiring-after-30-years); [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/how-to-invest/famous-investors/stanley-druckenmiller) *(attribution approximate)*
- **1992 GBP short ("Black Wednesday"):** Druckenmiller and Soros shorted sterling and reputedly made **more than $1B**. One account says the position grew to **$10B**. The $1B figure is a widely reported estimate, not a disclosed fund figure. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Druckenmiller); [3009 Substack](https://3009.substack.com/p/stanley-druckenmiller)
- **Career timeline:** joined Soros's Quantum Fund in 1988. Left in 2000 after large losses in technology stocks. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Druckenmiller)

#### Bruce Kovner (Caxton Associates): global macro; FX, interest-rate and commodity futures
- **Returns:** Caxton reportedly made about **$3B in profits in its first ten years, a gross trading return of 55.6%/yr**. It later grew to about $12B AUM with an **average net annual return above 21%**. Both figures come from a broker article and are unverified against fund records. **Grade C/D.** — [Dukascopy](https://www.dukascopy.com/swiss/english/marketwatch/articles/top-10-most-successful-forex-traders-in-the-world/)
- **Unverified Commodities Corporation figure:** a blog claims annual returns of nearly 80% while he was at Commodities Corporation. **Grade D, do not rely on it.** — [Beehiiv](https://prosperity.beehiiv.com/p/how-a-taxi-driver-became-a-billionaire)
- **Origin:** his first trade, in 1977, was a soybean futures position funded with **$3,000 borrowed on a credit card** (one source says $20,000). He later said it taught him the value of risk management. — [Dukascopy](https://www.dukascopy.com/swiss/english/marketwatch/articles/top-10-most-successful-forex-traders-in-the-world/); [Wikipedia: Bruce Kovner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Kovner)
- **Risk management (primary source: Kovner's Caxton 20th-anniversary note):**
  - Caxton chose to **target risk levels, not nominal dollar levels**, when calibrating trade size.
  - It used **dynamic risk allocation**, putting more capital at risk when conditions suggested excess return.
  - The note gives no per-trade percentage. — [brucekovner.com, Caxton 20th Anniversary](https://brucekovner.com/Caxton20thAnniversary.html)
- **Lineage:** Kovner was mentored by Michael Marcus. — [Wikipedia: Bruce Kovner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Kovner); [AAO Research Substack](https://aaoresearch.substack.com/p/breaking-it-down-bruce-kovner)

#### Michael Marcus (Commodities Corporation): discretionary futures, commodities and currencies
- **Record:** widely repeated as turning **$30,000 into $80M**. A Schwager *Market Wizards* excerpt says he multiplied his company account by **about 2,500-fold over ten years**. Sources disagree on the timespan: ten years vs about two decades. **Grade B** (Schwager interview, not an audit). — [TradeThePool, Market Wizards excerpt](https://tradethepool.com/trading-books/market-wizards-book-jack-schwager/?read=1); [TurtleTrader](https://www.turtletrader.com/trader-marcus); [Picture Perfect Portfolios](https://pictureperfectportfolios.com/how-to-invest-like-michael-marcus-commodity-trading-wizard/)
- **Firm and lineage:** traded the firm's own capital at Commodities Corporation. Described as a protégé of Ed Seykota and Amos Hostetter. — [Wikipedia: Michael Marcus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Marcus_(trader)); [TurtleTrader](https://www.turtletrader.com/trader-marcus)

#### Bill Lipschutz (Salomon Brothers FX; Hathersage Capital): FX specialist, G10 currencies
- **Salomon Brothers:** principal trader for Salomon's proprietary FX account from 1984 until June 1990. Appointed a Director in 1988. — [Wikipedia mirror (2007)](https://wikipedia2007.classicistranieri.com/en/b/i/l/Bill_Lipschutz_7245.html); [finnotes](https://finnotes.org/people/bill-lipschutz)
- **The "$300M" figure: sources conflict.** One says "by 1985, he was pulling in $300 million per year for Salomon". Others give a different year, or describe it as a cumulative "steady $300 million". **Grade C/D: the amount agrees across sources, but the year and whether it was annual do not.** — [Wikipedia mirror](https://wikipedia2007.classicistranieri.com/en/b/i/l/Bill_Lipschutz_7245.html); [TurtleTrader](https://www.turtletrader.com/?p=1951)
- **Personal account:** reportedly grew $12K to $250K while at Cornell. — [TurtleTrader](https://www.turtletrader.com/?p=1951)
- **Hathersage Capital Management:** founded in 1995 with Cornell classmates. A macro manager that **trades only G10 currencies**, described in a Trader magazine piece as a ~$200M FX vehicle. — [finnotes](https://finnotes.org/people/bill-lipschutz); [Metalsmine thread reproducing "Married to the Markets"](https://www.metalsmine.com/thread/9078-married-to-the-markets) *(attribution approximate)*
- **Featured in Schwager's *The New Market Wizards*.** — [finnotes](https://finnotes.org/people/bill-lipschutz)

#### Andrew Krieger (Bankers Trust, 1987): FX and FX options
- **The 1987 NZD short:** after Black Monday, Krieger, then 32 and at Bankers Trust, used currency options to take a short position against the New Zealand dollar worth hundreds of millions of dollars. — [The Star (Kenya)](https://www.the-star.co.ke/business/2020-11-26-the-biggest-forex-trades-ever-made); [TechRound](https://techround.co.uk/?p=59933)
- **Market impact:**
  - The kiwi reportedly fell 3–5%.
  - Legendary accounts claim his orders exceeded New Zealand's money supply. **This is anecdotal.**
  - A New Zealand official reportedly called Bankers Trust to get him out of the trade. — [NST Malaysia](https://www.nst.com.my/amp/opinion/columnists/2017/10/286072/understating-forex-losses-whose-orders); [The Star](https://www.the-star.co.ke/business/2020-11-26-the-biggest-forex-trades-ever-made)
- **The ~$300M profit figure** appears in only one broker article. **Grade D.** — [Dukascopy](https://www.dukascopy.com/swiss/english/marketwatch/articles/top-10-most-successful-forex-traders-in-the-world/)
- **Later career:** went on to work for George Soros. — [TechRound](https://techround.co.uk/?p=59933)

### Inferences
- **Timeframe:** these macro traders hold positions for days to months. The search results stated no explicit holding periods, so this is inferred from the nature of the trades: a crash short, a currency-peg break and post-crash currency moves.
- **Common pattern:** a fundamental or macro thesis decides direction. Price and technical tools decide timing and exits: Jones's 200-DMA and 1929 analog. Size is driven by asymmetric payoff: Jones's 5:1, and the reported $10B Soros/Druckenmiller sterling position.
- **Relevance to the user's markets:** Lipschutz and Krieger are the most purely FX-relevant. Jones is the most relevant to index futures (his 1987 S&P short) and is a clean rules source (200-DMA filter, 5:1). Kovner's "size to risk, not notional" is the most transferable risk rule.
- **Verification:** none of these records is independently audited in public. Schwager interviews (grade B) are the best available evidence for Marcus, Lipschutz and Jones.

### Gaps
- **Soros, beyond the 1992 trade:** no search returned his theory of reflexivity (*The Alchemy of Finance*, 1987) or Quantum Fund long-run returns. *UNVERIFIED background:* Quantum is often cited at roughly 30% annualised over about three decades.
- **Paul Tudor Jones:**
  - The search did not confirm that he used **Elliott Wave**. *UNVERIFIED background:* he discussed Elliott Wave and the 1929 analog in Schwager's *Market Wizards* (1989) and in the 1987 PBS documentary *Trader*.
  - No source was found for the "no losing year" claim about the Tudor BVI Global Fund.
  - *UNVERIFIED background on his rules:* "Don't average losers"; cut size when trading poorly; "Losers average losers"; always think about defense first.
- **Druckenmiller risk rules:** no source was retrieved. *UNVERIFIED background:* he credits Soros with teaching him that what matters is how much you make when right versus how much you lose when wrong, so concentrate size when conviction is high. He also focuses on central-bank liquidity as the main driver of markets.
- **Tom Baldwin (CBOT bond pit):** no search possible. *UNVERIFIED background:* interviewed in *The New Market Wizards* (1992). Reportedly started with about $25,000 in 1982 and became the largest individual local in the Treasury-bond futures pit. A pure order-flow scalper who read the pit and held positions for minutes to hours. No audited record.
- **Jim Rogers:** no search possible. *UNVERIFIED background:* co-founded the Quantum Fund with Soros in 1973. The fund is commonly reported as gaining about 4,200% over 1973–1980, against about 47% for the S&P 500. Launched the Rogers International Commodity Index in 1998. A long-horizon commodities and macro investor (years), not a short-term trader.
- **John Paulson (gold):** no search possible. *UNVERIFIED background:* became famous for the 2007 subprime CDS trade (about $15B for the firm). Made large gold bets from about 2009, including a GLD stake and a gold-denominated share class and gold fund. The gold fund suffered heavy losses in 2013, commonly reported at about -60%+. Converted to a family office in about 2020. This is a cautionary gold example, not a model.
- **Kovner:** his Forex-specific positions and any per-trade stop rule were not found.
- **Krieger:** no contemporaneous press source confirmed the profit figure.

## 2. Trend followers and systematic traders: records and rules

### Takeaway
Ed Seykota, Richard Dennis's Turtles and Jerry Parker (Chesapeake) are the archetypes of rules-based trend following across diversified futures (currencies, gold, stock indices, bonds, commodities). They used breakout and moving-average entries, volatility-based position sizing, and holding periods of weeks to months. Their headline records are grade B. Published Turtle rules have reportedly degraded markedly since the mid-1980s, according to backtests cited on Wikipedia.

### Cited Findings

#### Ed Seykota: systematic trend follower, diversified futures
- **Record:** according to Schwager's *Market Wizards*, he grew one client account **from $5,000 to $15,000,000 in 12 years**. That is a single account, not an audited composite. A Futures Magazine note quoted by one source puts career returns at **~60% net of fees over three decades**. **Grade B/D.** — [TurtleTrader: Seykota](https://www.turtletrader.com/trader-seykota); [Wikipedia: Ed Seykota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Seykota); [The Short Bear Substack](https://theshortbear.substack.com/p/ed-seykoda) *(attribution approximate)*
- **The often-quoted "250,000%" figure was not found** in any search result.
- **Method:**
  - Influenced by Richard Donchian's mechanical trend following and by Lefèvre's *Reminiscences of a Stock Operator*.
  - **First system was based on exponential moving averages**, described as a successor to Donchian channel breakouts with MAs replacing price channels.
  - He ran it every night and placed orders the next morning.
  - **Holding period: weeks to months.** — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Seykota); [DayTrading.com](https://www.daytrading.com/ed-seykota); [Habrador](https://blog.habrador.com/2011/08/ed-seykota-trading-system.html)
- **Risk and sizing:** positions were **sized on market volatility**. Later versions added pattern triggers and money-management algorithms. — [DayTrading.com](https://www.daytrading.com/ed-seykota); [MQL5 article](https://www.mql5.com/en/articles/24279) *(attribution approximate)*
- **Industry first and philosophy:** he conceived **the first commercial computerized trading system for managing client money in futures**. He held that a trader should build a system compatible with themselves rather than constantly change it. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Seykota); [TurtleTrader](https://www.turtletrader.com/trader-seykota)

#### Richard Dennis and the Turtle Traders: breakout trend following, diversified futures
- **The experiment:** Dennis and William Eckhardt ran it to settle whether trading is innate or teachable. — [Wikipedia: Richard Dennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Dennis); [CME Group education](https://www.cmegroup.com/education/the-turtle-traders-featuring-richard-dennis)
- **Record: the timeframe conflicts.** Wikipedia says the Turtles "reportedly" earned **$175M aggregate** by the end of the experiment. Former Turtle Russell Sands, via a Substack post, says **$175M in 4 years, about 80% compounded**. **Grade C/D (reported, not audited).** — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Dennis); [The Short Bear Substack](https://theshortbear.substack.com/p/richard-dennis)
- **Method:** a precise trend-following system **buying upside breakouts and selling downside breakdowns**. The full rules have been published in at least two books. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Dennis)
- **Edge decay:** backtests show a **drastic drop in performance after 1986 and roughly flat results from 1996 to 2009**. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Dennis)
- **After the experiment:** several Turtles, including Parker, ran CTA businesses using techniques similar but not identical to the Turtle system. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Dennis); [Nasdaq: The Turtle Trading Story](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/the-turtle-trading-story-2010-08-17)

#### Jerry Parker (Chesapeake Capital): Turtle-derived long-term trend following
- **Background:** a 25-year-old public accountant in Virginia when he answered Dennis's ad. **Founded Chesapeake Capital Management in 1988.** One source claims a peak of $2.5B AUM, which is unverified. — [TurtleTrader: Jerry Parker](https://www.turtletrader.com/?p=967); [Wikipedia: Richard Dennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Dennis)

### Inferences
- **Category:** these are the "quant/systematic + indicator" archetype. Entries come from price-channel breakouts (Donchian/Turtles) or EMA crossovers (Seykota). Sizing is volatility-normalised, and holding periods run weeks to months.
- **Markets:** all trade FX, gold and stock-index futures as part of a diversified basket. None specialises in a single market.
- **Lesson for strategy design:** the post-1986 decay in published Turtle rules is a documented warning. Publicly known breakout rules lose edge, so robustness testing and diversification matter more than the exact parameters.

### Gaps
- **Turtle rule specifics and Curtis Faith:** no source was retrieved. *UNVERIFIED background, from Curtis Faith's "The Original Turtle Trading Rules" and *Way of the Turtle* (2007):*
  - **System 1:** 20-day breakout entry, 10-day opposite-breakout exit.
  - **System 2:** 55-day breakout entry, 20-day exit.
  - Volatility unit "N" is the 20-day ATR. One unit risks 1% of equity per 1N move.
  - Initial stop at 2N. Pyramid up to 4 units at ½N intervals.
  - Unit limits: 4 per market, 6 in closely correlated markets, 10 in loosely correlated markets, 12 per direction.
  - Faith, the youngest Turtle at 19, reportedly made more than $31M for Dennis.
- **Richard Dennis's personal record:** not retrieved. *UNVERIFIED background:* reportedly turned about $1,600 to $5,000 into roughly $200M. Suffered heavy losses in 1987–88.
- **John W. Henry (JWH):** no search possible. *UNVERIFIED background:* long-term systematic trend follower, including a "Financial and Metals Portfolio" covering FX, rates, metals and stock indices. Stopped managing outside client money in about 2012. Principal owner of the Boston Red Sox.
- **Bill Dunn (Dunn Capital):** no search possible. *UNVERIFIED background:* long-term systematic trend follower since the 1970s, with the WMA program running since 1984. Known for strict risk-based position sizing and very large but recovered drawdowns.
- **David Harding (AHL co-founder 1987, Winton founded 1997):** no search possible. *UNVERIFIED background:* research-driven systematic CTA. Winton later reduced its reliance on pure trend following.
- **Jim Simons (Renaissance Medallion, quant context only):** no search possible. *UNVERIFIED background:* Gregory Zuckerman's *The Man Who Solved the Market* (2019) reports about 66% gross / 39% net annual returns for Medallion over 1988–2018. Short-term statistical-arbitrage signals; closed to outside investors. Simons died in May 2024.
- **The "250,000% over 16 years" Seykota figure:** not confirmed in this session.

## 3. Trading-competition champions: World Cup Trading Championships (WCTC/"Robbins Cup") 1984–2026 and the US Investing Championship (USIC)

### Takeaway
The WCTC (World Cup Championship of Futures Trading®, now run as World Cup Trading Championships®) is the main long-running real-money futures contest. Larry Williams's **1987 11,376%** is its most-cited result. Michelle Williams (1997, ~1,000%) and four-time champion **Andrea Unger** (2008, 2009, 2010, 2012) are its other famous winners. Recent winners include **Ivan Scherman (2023)**, confirmed by the CMT Association and the WCTC's X account; the reported 491% is from unofficial sites. The **2024 winner conflicts** between sources (Brent Carlile at 532.3% vs Ryan LaVelle at 138.6%). **No confirmed 2025 champion was found**, and the 2025–2026 Global Cup closes on 31 Dec 2026. Competition returns are produced by deliberately extreme risk and are not investable track records.

### Cited Findings

#### Competition structure and verification
- **The official site** is worldcupchampionships.com. It dates the contest to **1983**, while other sources say 1984. — [World Cup Trading Championships®](https://www.worldcupchampionships.com/); [Trading Tournaments history](https://trading-tournaments.com/articles/wctc-champions-year-by-year)
- **An unofficial year-by-year table (1984–2025)** covers the Futures, Forex and Global Cup divisions. It notes that **the 1988 result is not consistently listed** in the public standings archive and that some division podiums are omitted. **There is no final 2026 champion yet:** the 2025–2026 Global Cup closes on **31 Dec 2026**. — [Trading Tournaments: WCTC champions year by year](https://trading-tournaments.com/articles/wctc-champions-year-by-year); [Trading Tournaments: WCTC 2026](https://trading-tournaments.com/tournaments/championships/wctc)
- **Conflicts across unofficial lists:**
  - **1984:** "Martin Schwartz" vs "Ralph Casazzone 264.30%".
  - **Chuck Hughes:** credited with ten titles across divisions on one page, but one win on another. He is listed for 2015 on one page and for 1999 on another.
  - **Stuart Walton (1989, 1991, 2000) and Mike Lundgren (1989, 1990, 1992)** each appear in only one source, and their years overlap.
  - **2000–2001:** Kurt Sakaeda is listed for 2000 and David Cash for 2001. The official site mentions a 2001 winner with a 53% return.
  - These pages appear to be partly auto-generated. — [Trading Tournaments hall of fame](https://trading-tournaments.com/hall-of-fame); [Trading Tournaments results](https://trading-tournaments.com/results); [Trading Tournaments by platform](https://trading-tournaments.com/traders/by-platform/wctc)

#### Larry Williams: 1987 WCTC champion; futures (see section 4 for methods)
- **1987: 11,376% net return** in the year-long World Cup Championship of Futures Trading. The CMT Association rounds it to ~11,300%. — [Trading Tournaments](https://trading-tournaments.com/articles/wctc-champions-year-by-year); [CMT Association](https://cmtassociation.org/?p=24881); [finance.cz](https://www.finance.cz/clanky/301893-larry-williams-10-000-za-rok-jako-jediny-na-svete/)
- **Verification controversy:**
  - One source says US regulators tried to prove fraud and failed.
  - Another says the organisers checked the results twice and initially withheld the prize because the result seemed impossible. It was ultimately accepted.
  - A third says the result **was never documented by publicly released account statements**.
  - **Grade A- (accepted by the contest), but disputed.** — [Comparic](https://comparic.pl/slawni-traderzy-larry-williams/); [finance.cz](https://www.finance.cz/clanky/301893-larry-williams-10-000-za-rok-jako-jediny-na-svete/) *(attribution approximate)*

#### Michelle Williams: 1997 WCTC champion (Larry's daughter, later an actress)
- **Won the 1997 WCTC with about a 1,000% return.** Her age is given as 16 in some sources and 17 in others. She was coached by her father. — [Trading Tournaments](https://trading-tournaments.com/articles/wctc-champions-year-by-year); [StockCharts / ChartWatchers](https://c.stockcharts.com/articles/chartwatchers/2023/03/trading-legend-larry-williams-118.html) *(attribution approximate)*

#### Andrea Unger (Italy): four-time WCTC futures champion; systematic futures
- **Record:**
  - **2008: 672% net**, the first Italian winner.
  - Further wins in **2009, 2010 and 2012**, making him the only four-time champion.
  - One page cites **240% in 2010**.
  - **Grade A** for the titles (contest records as reported). The specific percentages come from secondary pages. — [fxstreet.cz](https://www.fxstreet.cz/tym-purple-trading-andrea-unger-svetovy-sampion-v-tradingu.html); [Wikitia](https://wikitia.com/wiki/Andrea_Unger); [Trading Tournaments: Unger](https://trading-tournaments.com/articles/andrea-unger-four-time-champion)
- **On competition returns:** such returns are "generated by specifically trying to win the comp, and taking on the associated risks". They are not a normal investment return. — [The Algorithmic Advantage Substack](https://thealgorithmicadvantage.substack.com/p/038-andrea-unger-672-returns-sure)
- **Method (his systematic approach, not necessarily the exact 2008 system):**
  - Start from **simple, robust ideas such as breakout or reversal setups**.
  - Add **context rules** derived from historical market conditions.
  - **Match the strategy type to the market's character:** trend following for trending markets such as crude oil, and **counter-trend/mean reversion for mean-reverting markets such as the E-mini S&P 500**.

## 4. Price-action and chart traders with long futures/FX/gold records: methods and records

### Takeaway
Peter Brandt (classical chart patterns on futures, including gold and FX), Linda Bradford Raschke (short-term futures swing setups: Holy Grail, Turtle Soup, 80-20) and Larry Williams (volatility breakouts) have the best-documented records among chart and price-action traders. Their figures come from Schwager books, a firm audit or self-reports (grades B/C). Popular price-action educators such as Al Brooks, Tom Hougaard and John Carter have no public audited track records and could not be researched in this session.

### Cited Findings

#### Peter L. Brandt (Factor Trading/Factor LLC): classical charting on futures, FX and gold
- **Background:** founded **Factor Trading Co. in 1980** (now Factor LLC). His 1990 book on classical chart patterns is widely regarded as a classic. — [PeterLBrandt.com: About](https://www.peterlbrandt.com/about-us/)
- **Track record: three different numbers.**

## 5. Strategy-category mapping (ICT/SMC, classical TA, indicators, order flow, macro, quant) and recurring risk rules

### Takeaway
No trader in this set with a verified or semi-verified record is documented as using ICT/SMC as such. The evidence-backed edges cluster in four places:
- macro/fundamental direction plus technical timing (Jones, Soros/Druckenmiller, Kovner, Lipschutz, Krieger)
- systematic trend following with breakouts or MAs (Seykota, the Turtles, Parker)
- short-term pattern and breakout or mean-reversion setups in futures (Williams, Raschke, Unger)
- classical chart patterns (Brandt)

Risk control, rather than the entry method, is the common denominator: asymmetric reward:risk, volatility- or risk-based sizing, and fast exits.

### Cited Findings
- **Jones:** 5:1 reward:risk; 200-DMA as both trend filter and exit; 1929 analog overlay. — [25iq](https://25iq.com/2015/07/25/a-dozen-things-ive-learned-from-paul-tudor-jones-about-investing-and-trading); [Felder Report](https://thefelderreport.com/2014/03/13/for-everyone-who-thinks-tom-demarks-1929-analog-is-a-joke)
- **Kovner:** size to targeted risk, not notional; dynamic risk allocation. — [Caxton 20th Anniversary](https://brucekovner.com/Caxton20thAnniversary.html)
- **Seykota:** EMA-based trend system with volatility-based sizing, holding for weeks to months. — [DayTrading.com](https://www.daytrading.com/ed-seykota); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Seykota)
- **Turtles:** breakout buying and breakdown selling; edge decayed after 1986. — [Wikipedia: Richard Dennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Dennis)
- **Unger:** breakout or reversal base logic; trend for crude, mean reversion for the E-mini S&P. — [Algorithmic Advantage](https://thealgorithmicadvantage.substack.com/p/038-andrea-unger-672-returns-sure)
- **Raschke:** Turtle Soup (failed-breakout reversal), 80-20 (intraday), Holy Grail. — [TurtleTrader](https://www.turtletrader.com/trader-raschke/); [RoboForex](https://roboforex.com/blog/education/trading-strategies-that-were-a-revolution-three-strategies-of-linda-raschke/)
