## 1. Auction Market Theory (AMT) and Market Profile (Steidlmayer, Dalton): TPO, value area, POC, initial balance, day types, open types, 80% rule

### Takeaway
Market Profile was a CBOT product (1985) built on J. Peter Steidlmayer's work. It shows each session as a time-at-price distribution: TPO letters per 30-minute period, a POC, and a value area holding about 70% of activity. James Dalton's *Mind Over Markets* later turned this into the "auction" reading that traders use now: day types classified by initial-balance size relative to the day's range, the four open types, and the 80% rule. Definitions are consistent across sources, but I found **no independent, peer-reviewed statistics** on IB-breakout or 80%-rule win rates for ES/NQ. The published numbers come from TradingView indicator authors and vendors.

### Cited Findings
**Origin and history**
- Market Profile is "an intra-day charting technique devised by J. Peter Steidlmayer, a trader at the Chicago Board of Trade (CBOT), ca 1959-1985." It was introduced to the public in 1985 as part of a CBOT product, the CBOT Market Profile (CBOTMP1), which paired the graphic with new data (the Liquidity Data Bank). — [Wikipedia: Market profile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_profile)
- The first Market Profile course was reportedly taught in 1987 at Western Illinois University by Prof. Thomas P. Drinka. Steidlmayer and Kevin Koy later started the "Logic Market School." — [Capital.com](https://capital.com/market-profile-definition)
- The "auction" reading (markets as ongoing two-way auctions seeking value) "was systematized by later educators, most influentially the Dalton tradition of *Mind Over Markets*." LuxAlgo dates the format to the 1980s CBOT, which conflicts with Wikipedia's "ca 1959" start for Steidlmayer's development work. — [LuxAlgo: TPO Profile](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/tpo-profile.md)
- Dalton's *Mind Over Markets* has dedicated chapters on Open-Drive, Open-Test-Drive, Open-Rejection-Reverse and Open-Auction. Only the table of contents was retrievable. — [Ex Libris listing, Mind Over Markets](https://www.exlibris.ch/de/buecher-buch/english-books/dalton-james-f-/mind-over-markets/id/9781118531730/)
- Reference material on the original Market Profile/Liquidity Data Bank is limited (1988 S&C article: "Market Profile/Liquidity Data Bank: A procedural outline" by Donald L. Jones). — [Traders.com store](https://store.traders.com/traderscom/-v06-c11-mrktpro-pdf.html)

**Mechanics: TPO, POC, value area, initial balance**
- TPO = Time Price Opportunity. The session is split into periods, usually 30 minutes, each assigned a letter, and every price touched in that period prints the letter once. The row with the most letters is the **POC**. The band holding about **70% of TPOs** is the **value area**. — [LuxAlgo: TPO Profile](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/tpo-profile.md)
- The 70% value area comes from a normal-distribution analogy: "the central seventy percent of trading activity about POC (+/- one standard deviation) is termed the 'Value Area'." — [Wikipedia: Market profile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_profile); [Market Profile Basics PDF (mql5 mirror)](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/117/market_profile_basics.pdf)
- The 70% is a convention, not a law. Platforms differ slightly (68% vs 70%). — [El Trader Financiado: Volume Profile](https://www.eltraderfinanciado.com/en/blog/volume-profile)
- **Initial balance (IB)** = the range set at the start of the regular session, classically the first hour, i.e. the first two 30-minute TPO periods. — [LuxAlgo: Initial Balance](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/initial-balance.md)

**Day types (classified by IB relative to day range)**
- Secondary sources list Non-Trend, Normal, Normal Variation, Trend and Neutral profiles. Rough IB-to-range ratios: **Normal day** IB ≈ 80% of the day's range; **Normal Variation** IB ≈ 50%; **Trend day** IB < 25% of the day's range. A **Neutral day** is defined by range extension in **both** directions and a close near the middle of the range. These ratios come from a 2004 trade-publication excerpt, and sources disagree on exact thresholds. — [Market Profile Basics PDF (mql5 mirror)](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/117/market_profile_basics.pdf); [Scribd: Market Profile](https://www.scribd.com/document/530897583/Market-Profile)

**Open types (Dalton)**
- **Open-Drive:** price moves directionally from the first prints and does not trade back through the open (highest conviction). **Open-Test-Drive:** the market probes a nearby reference or the other side of the open, finds no business, then drives the other way (somewhat less conviction). **Open-Rejection-Reverse:** an early move meets opposing activity and returns back through the open. A typical case is opening outside prior value, extending further away, then reversing through the opening price. **Open-Auction:** price rotates around the open with no early conviction, most often inside prior-day value. The conventional ordering is from most to least initial conviction. An open drive does **not** guarantee a trend day. — [LuxAlgo: Open Types](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/open-types/); [NexusFi: Open Type Classification](https://nexusfi.com/a/concepts/open-type-classification); [Marketcalls: Open type and confidence](https://www.marketcalls.in/market-profile/market-profile-open-type-and-confidence.html)

**The 80% rule**
- This is a mean-reversion setup. Price opens **outside** the prior day's value area, then trades back **inside** and holds. The classic trigger is **two consecutive 30-minute TPO periods inside the value area**, then entry toward the **opposite** value-area boundary. Screen-based traders now often look for "acceptance" (time and volume built inside the VA) rather than a quick probe. — [LuxAlgo: 80 percent rule](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/80-percent-rule/)

**IB breakout/extension statistics (practitioner only)**
- Published ES/NQ IB-extension percentages from an independent dataset were not found. TradingView indicator descriptions say: (a) one indicator's break/pullback/target probabilities come from a 3-year NQ backtest (Jan 2023–Dec 2025, 614 trading days), and its author warns these do **not** transfer to ES, YM, forex or crypto; (b) another cites 12 years of 1-minute NQ data (2013–2025) and tracks which IB side breaks first; (c) one cites a "TradingStats" 12-year NQ IB study for continuation rates at 1.1×–1.5× IB extensions, noting these are reach rates, not win rates. I could not tell which claim belongs to which script. — [TradingView: Initial Balance Extensions](https://www.tradingview.com/script/lR2POs1Y-Initial-Balance-Extensions); [TradingView: Initial Balance Statistical Mapping](https://www.tradingview.com/script/2iuvi66N-Initial-Balance-Statistical-Mapping/); [TradingView: Nick's IB Break Extension Retest v3](https://br.tradingview.com/script/xB4JV02s-Nick-s-IB-Break-Extension-Retest-v3/)

### Inferences
- **How the 80% rule is traded (inference from the definition above):** Entry comes after the second consecutive 30-minute bracket closes inside prior VA, on the side away from the open. The target is the opposite VAH/VAL. A logical stop is back outside the VA edge that price re-entered, since a failure there means acceptance outside value. The name implies an ~80% completion rate, but no source here verifies that rate for ES/NQ. Treat it as a label, not a statistic.
- **Open types as a day-type filter:** Open-Drive and Open-Test-Drive favour trend or "normal variation" days and IB-extension trades. Open-Auction inside prior value favours rotational (normal/neutral) days, VA-edge fades and POC targets. This is a reasonable synthesis of Dalton's conviction ordering, not a tested rule.
- **IB size as a regime tell:** By the day-type ratios above, a narrow IB (small relative to recent average range) raises the odds of range extension and trend-day behaviour. A wide IB (≈80% of a typical day's range) points to a normal day where fading IB extremes makes more sense.
- Because the only ES/NQ IB statistics found are indicator-vendor claims on short samples (e.g., 614 NQ days), anyone deploying IB strategies on NQ/ES/MNQ/MES should backtest on their own RTH 9:30–10:30 ET data.

### Gaps
- The full text of Dalton's *Mind Over Markets* and Steidlmayer's own publications (*Markets and Market Logic*, 1986, title not verified this session) could not be accessed. Exact Dalton definitions of **single prints, poor highs/lows, excess, P/b/D-shaped profiles, double-distribution days and overnight inventory** were **not** retrieved with sources this session.
  - Commonly taught, not verified this session: *single prints* are one-TPO-wide areas from fast directional moves, often revisited. *Excess* is a tail of single prints at an extreme, read as a completed auction. A *poor high/low* is a flat extreme with no excess, seen as unfinished business likely revisited. *P-shape* means short covering/buying with value forming high. *b-shape* means long liquidation with value forming low. *D-shape* means balance. *Double distribution* is two separate value areas joined by single prints. *Overnight inventory* means net long/short positioning from the overnight session versus the prior settlement, which tends to be "corrected" early in RTH when lopsided.
- No peer-reviewed or exchange-published statistics on IB break frequency (one side vs both sides), 1.5×/2× IB extensions, or 80%-rule completion rates for ES/NQ/GC were found.
- No sourced statistics on open-type frequency or follow-through for ES/NQ were found.

## 2. Volume Profile: POC, VAH/VAL, HVN/LVN, naked POCs, composite/session/developing/fixed-range profiles

### Takeaway
Volume Profile applies the Market Profile idea to traded volume at each price instead of time. Traders read HVNs (high-volume nodes, "accepted" prices where price slows) as magnets or support and resistance. LVNs (low-volume nodes, "rejected" prices crossed quickly) mark where price either accelerates or gets rejected sharply. Naked (unrevisited) POCs serve as targets. Sources are educational or vendor material, and **no rigorous effectiveness study** was found.

### Cited Findings
- **HVN** is a "thick" zone with heavy trading that tends to slow price. **LVN** is a thin zone that price crossed quickly. A **naked POC** is a prior-session POC not yet revisited. When price returns to an LVN it "often either accelerates through or gets rejected sharply." The 70% VA is a convention (68% or 70% depending on platform). The same article lists three tradable rules: the 80% rule, the failed auction, and the IB breakout. — [El Trader Financiado: Volume Profile](https://www.eltraderfinanciado.com/en/blog/volume-profile)
- A TradingView composite-profile indicator builds prior-day, prior-week and full-history composite levels with the same value-area routine. It explicitly plots context and does **not** generate buy/sell signals. Another script description says LVN reaction direction depends on context, not the LVN alone. — [TradingView scripts: Volume Profile Composite, Naked POC & Value Area](https://tw.tradingview.com/scripts/point/); [TradingView: volume profile multitimeframe](https://in.tradingview.com/script/pTF1Asiv-volume-profil-multitimeframe/)
- **Trader Dale** (a popular retail Volume Profile/Order Flow educator) teaches combining Order Flow with Volume Profile to find "institutional" support and resistance and to set stops and targets. His books are *Volume Profile: The Insider's Guide to Trading* and *Order Flow: Trading Setups*. — [Shortform summary: Order Flow by Trader Dale](https://www.shortform.com/pdf/order-flow-pdf-trader-dale); [Blackwell's: Order Flow by Trader Dale](https://blackwells.co.uk/bookshop/product/Order-Flow-by-Trader-Dale/9798591281175); [Webinar summary: How to Trade with Volume Profile in 2025](https://videohighlight.com/v/FGAmaUCOlLM)
- ATAS (an order-flow/volume platform) runs an education section on where volume analysis works, i.e., centralized exchange markets with real volume. — [ATAS Learn: Where It Works](https://learn.atas.net/volume-basics/volume-analysis/where-it-works)

### Inferences
- **Typical setups (inference from the definitions):**
  1. *LVN rejection:* in a trend, price pulls back into an LVN left by the impulse leg. The entry is a rejection there (ideally confirmed by footprint absorption or a delta flip), the stop goes beyond the LVN/next HVN, and the target is the prior swing or the next HVN/naked POC.
  2. *Value-area rotation:* on balanced days, fade VAH/VAL toward the POC, or run the 80% rule (VAL to VAH or VAH to VAL).
  3. *Naked POC magnet:* prior-session naked POCs act as targets for rotational moves and as reaction levels.
  4. *Composite profile* (multi-day/week) gives the bigger balance area. A breakout from composite balance that holds outside (acceptance) suggests a new trend leg.
- The **fixed-range profile** applies the same logic to an arbitrary swing (e.g., the last impulse leg) to locate its internal LVNs and POC. The **developing (session) profile** shows in real time whether value is migrating up, down or overlapping.
- Since volume profile works best with true centralized exchange volume, it suits CME products (ES/NQ/GC and micros) much better than spot FX (see Section 8).

### Gaps
- No academic or large-sample independent backtest of HVN/LVN or naked-POC strategies on ES/NQ/GC was found.
- Platform-specific behaviour (e.g., Sierra Chart TPO/VbP study settings, ATAS cluster/profile options) could not be retrieved this session.

## 3. VWAP, anchored VWAP (Brian Shannon), VWAP standard-deviation bands: reversion vs trend

### Takeaway
The session VWAP is the main intraday "fair value" reference for ES/NQ, and ES's own daily settlement is a 30-second VWAP. Brian Shannon popularized **anchored VWAP (AVWAP)**: VWAP started from a meaningful event (swing low/high, earnings, gap) to gauge who controls the move since then. The strongest quantitative evidence is a pair of Zarattini/Aziz (Concretum/Bear Bull Traders) papers showing VWAP-based **trend-following** on QQQ/SPY was profitable in backtests. These are author-promoted, and independent replications look weaker. Practitioner sources agree that the key decision is **regime first** (rotational vs trend day): fade ±2σ bands only on balanced days, and ride bands on trend days.

### Cited Findings
**Anchored VWAP (Brian Shannon)**
- Shannon calls the anchored VWAP the "most accurate, objective measurement of supply and demand." It combines price, volume and time from the anchor forward. He describes buying strength after a dip once buyers are back in control, with stops below the most recent or relevant lows. The line is read like a moving average (its slope and price's distance from it both matter). — [Benzinga: Brian Shannon's Favorite Ways to Use the Anchored VWAP](https://www.benzinga.com/general/education/22/05/27200039/my-favorite-ways-to-use-the-anchored-vwap-brian-shannon)
- In the "VWAP hand-off" variant (community script based on Shannon), a new AVWAP is plotted each time price taps the previous one, and the sequence reveals trend and future pivots. "Messy, tangled VWAPs suggest choppy price action with no trend yet." — [TradingView: Anchored VWAP Hand-Off](https://jp.tradingview.com/script/9wtLN3Mf-Anchored-VWAP-Hand-Off)

**Evidence: VWAP trend-following (Zarattini & Aziz)**
- *"Volume Weighted Average Price (VWAP): The Holy Grail for Day Trading Systems"* (Zarattini & Aziz, SSRN 4631351, Nov 2023): go long when price is above VWAP and short when below. Test period Jan 2, 2018 – Sep 28, 2023. **QQQ:** $25,000 → $192,656 net of commissions (**+671%**), max drawdown **9.4%**, Sharpe **2.1**, versus QQQ buy-and-hold **+126%**, 37% max DD, Sharpe 0.7. **TQQQ:** $25,000 → $2,085,417 (**+8,242%**) with a comparable max DD. — [Concretum Group: VWAP paper](https://concretumgroup.com/volume-weighted-average-price-vwap-the-holy-grail-for-day-trading-systems/); [Bear Bull Traders: Magic of VWAP](https://bearbulltraders.com/?p=2606339)
- Caveat: one QuantConnect reproduction notes that several public QuantConnect replications reported far lower numbers and concluded "there's no holy grail here" (single source, not independently verified). — [QuantConnect backtest](https://www.quantconnect.com/terminal/cache/embedded_backtest_0cab7e083e99d5a372e02fe882bbf078.html)
- *"Beat the Market: An Effective Intraday Momentum Strategy for S&P500 ETF (SPY)"* (Zarattini, Aziz, Barbon; SFI Research Paper 24-97): opens trend-following positions as soon as there is "abnormal demand/supply imbalance" in intraday price action, with dynamic trailing stops. Backtest 2007 – early 2024: **total return 1,985% net of costs, 19.6% annualized, Sharpe 1.33.** Tested across volatility regimes, day-of-week and **dealer gamma imbalance**, with commissions and slippage modelled. — [Swiss Finance Institute: N°24-97](https://www.sfi.ch/fr/publications/n-24-97-beat-the-market-an-effective-intraday-momentum-strategy-for-s-p500-etf-spy); [IDEAS/RePEc rp2497](https://ideas.repec.org/p/chf/rpseri/rp2497.html)
- The "noise area" in that paper: bands = day's open ± the average absolute move from the open to the same minute-of-day over recent days, adjusted for overnight gaps. Per an indicator recreation (not the paper itself), lookback is 14 days with a volatility multiplier of 1. A break outside the band signals a possible imbalance that may start an intraday trend. — [TradingView: Concretum Bands](https://it.tradingview.com/script/CUpWCZhe-Concretum-Bands)

**VWAP bands: reversion vs trend (practitioner)**
- LuxAlgo frames session VWAP as supporting two opposite playbooks: **classify the regime first, then pick the rule set.** Classifiers include which side of VWAP price has held, VWAP slope, number of crossings, band width, or z-score distance. Regimes can flip mid-session, and misclassifying the day is the main way both playbooks lose. — [LuxAlgo: VWAP Mean-reversion vs Trend Regimes](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/vwap-mean-reversion-vs-trend-regimes.md); [LuxAlgo: VWAP Bands](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/vwap-bands.md)
- Mean reversion reportedly works best on ES/NQ on non-event days, especially in lunch and early-afternoon chop. The usual setup fades a ~2× session standard-deviation move from VWAP with VWAP as the target. One source claims an unfiltered win rate of "closer to 45%" with no supporting backtest. On trend days price can ride an outer band for hours. Suggested filters: skip FOMC/CPI/NFP days; ADX > 25 on 5-min bars flags trend; a gap beyond prior value on elevated volume leans trend, while an open inside prior VA that crosses back over VWAP leans rotational; use RTH only; ignore bands in the first minutes (too few observations). — [CrossTrade: VWAP reversion](https://crosstrade.io/learn/trading-strategies/vwap-reversion); [NexusFi: VWAP Standard Deviation Bands](https://nexusfi.com/a/indicators/vwap-standard-deviation-bands); [Topstep Brokerage: 3 indicators ES traders need to watch](https://topstepbrokerage.com/blog/3-indicators-es-traders-need-to-watch)
- The NexusFi claim that ±2 SD reversion is "empirically strong across thousands of ES and NQ sessions" cites no study. — [NexusFi: VWAP Standard Deviation Bands](https://nexusfi.com/a/indicators/vwap-standard-deviation-bands)
- ES daily settlement is itself a VWAP: the VWAP of all CME Globex trades between 14:59:30 and 15:00:00 CT in the lead month, rounded to the nearest 0.25 point. — [CME: E-mini S&P 500 / Micro E-mini Daily Settlement Procedure](https://cmegroup.com/education/articles-and-reports/faq-daily-settlement-price-determination-time-change.html); [CME client wiki: settlement procedure](https://cmegroupclientsite.atlassian.net/wiki/x/E4DaBw)

### Inferences
- **Two VWAP playbooks for ES/NQ:**
  - **Trend (evidence-backed on ETFs):** stay on the side of VWAP that price holds. Enter on pullbacks to VWAP or the 1σ band in the trend direction, trail a stop at VWAP, and exit by the close. This is close to the Zarattini/Aziz logic.
  - **Reversion (practitioner only):** on balanced/rotational days (open inside prior value, flat VWAP, multiple crosses), fade ±2σ toward VWAP with a stop beyond ±3σ or the session extreme.
- **AVWAP anchors commonly used by futures traders:** prior-day RTH open, overnight high/low, the 8:30 ET data-release bar, weekly/monthly opens, major swing pivots, FOMC bar. Several AVWAPs converging marks a "consensus cost basis" zone.
- The QQQ/SPY results are ETF backtests. Transferring them to NQ/ES (or the micros) adds futures roll, ETH/RTH session-definition choices and different cost structures. Real-world edge is likely materially below the headline numbers.

### Gaps
- Shannon's book (*Maximum Trading Gains with Anchored VWAP*, 2023, title not verified this session) and his exact rule set were not retrieved.
- No independent, peer-reviewed out-of-sample test of VWAP band reversion on ES/NQ futures was found.
- No sourced study of VWAP behaviour in gold futures (GC/MGC) was found.

## 4. Order flow: footprint, delta and cumulative delta, absorption, exhaustion, imbalances, icebergs, spoofing, DOM/tape, Bookmap; educators and platforms

### Takeaway
Order-flow trading reads **executed aggressive volume** (footprint/bid-ask volume, delta, cumulative delta) and **resting liquidity** (DOM, Bookmap heatmap, icebergs) to judge who is in control at key auction/volume-profile locations. Signal definitions (absorption, exhaustion, stacked imbalances, delta divergence) are consistent across vendors. **No academic or independent evidence of profitability** was found, and published win-rate claims (e.g., "70–80%") are unsupported marketing. Spoofing is real and prosecuted (the JPMorgan precious-metals case, $920.2M), which is exactly why DOM/heatmap readers distrust displayed liquidity.

### Cited Findings
**Signal definitions (footprint/delta)**
- **Stacked imbalances:** one side dominates by a set multiple (e.g., 300%) across several consecutive price levels. This marks initiative, leaves intrabar support/resistance, and is usually read as continuation, with the levels watched on retests. **Absorption:** heavy aggressive volume fails to move price because passive limit orders absorb it, often leading to reversal or a trap. **Exhaustion:** a new high/low prints on a bar where the delta gain is smaller than the prior bar's, with previously dominant imbalances fading. It is a warning, not a standalone signal. **Delta divergence:** price and delta point in different directions (e.g., negative delta while price holds or rises = bullish). — [LuxAlgo: Footprint Concepts](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/footprint-concepts/); [NexusFi: Advanced Footprint Chart Patterns](https://nexusfi.com/a/strategies/advanced-footprint-chart-patterns); [TradeZella: Footprint charts](https://www.tradezella.com/learning-items/footprint-charts)
- Footprint signals depend on accurate buy/sell (aggressor) classification of trades. — [LuxAlgo: Footprint Concepts](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/footprint-concepts/)
- A TradingView script claims a 70–80% win rate on such setups with no evidence. Treat this as unsupported. — [TradingView: Advanced Footprint Analysis](https://de.tradingview.com/script/7S1vHl1Z-Advanced-Footprint-Analysis)

**Liquidity: Bookmap, icebergs, spoofing**
- Bookmap's heatmap plots every order-book change (placements, cancellations, executions). Per Bookmap, "liquidity holding = potential support/resistance; liquidity pulling = potential breakout or shift." Its heatmap separates orders that execute from orders that vanish before execution, which helps expose spoof orders. — [Bookmap: Why Bookmap](https://bookmap.com/why-bookmap); [Bookmap blog: Trade via Bookmap step-by-step](https://bookmap.com/blog/trade-via-bookmap-detailed-step-step-guide)
- Bookmap's iceberg/stop detection is a paid add-on. A futures-broker guide says iceberg and spoof detection need **MBO (market-by-order)** data, which aggregated (MBP) feeds don't provide (broker marketing). A forum user argues that tracking stacking/pulling across many levels by watching a heatmap is "virtually impossible" (opinion). — [NexusFi: Bookmap + CQG futures liquidity trading](https://nexusfi.com/a/platforms/bookmap-cqg-futures-liquidity-trading); [NexusFi forum thread](https://nexusfi.com/showthread.php?p=840007)
- **Spoofing enforcement, relevant to gold:** On Sept 29, 2020 the CFTC ordered JPMorgan to pay **$920.2M**, the largest CFTC monetary relief ever at the time. Spoofing ran for at least eight years and involved "hundreds of thousands" of spoof orders in **precious-metals and U.S. Treasury futures** across three exchanges. The total was made up of a civil penalty ≈$436.4M, restitution ≈$311.7M and disgorgement ≈$172M, plus a DOJ deferred prosecution agreement. — [CFTC Release 8260-20](https://cftc.gov/node/233596); [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/jpmorgan-pays-920-million-spoofing-claims-197541); [CoinWeek](https://coinweek.com/people-in-the-news/crime-and-fraud/cftc-orders-jpmorgan-to-pay-record-920-million-for-precious-metals-spoofing-manipulation/)
- Navinder Singh Sarao, a British day trader accused of contributing to the 2010 Flash Crash, is described as "the most infamous spoofer." He spoofed in E-mini S&P futures. — [Mining.com](https://www.mining.com/web/jpmorgans-big-hitters-of-gold-market-face-trial-over-spoofing/)

### Inferences
- **Catalogue of order-flow setups (synthesized from definitions; not tested):**
  1. **Absorption at a level:** at a prior VAH/VAL, naked POC, IB extreme or overnight high/low, heavy aggressive selling prints into a bid that doesn't break (large bid volume on footprint, price unchanged). Go long when price lifts off the level. Stop a few ticks beyond the absorption price; target VWAP/POC or the opposite side of value.
  2. **Exhaustion/failed auction:** a new extreme with shrinking delta and thin volume at the very high/low (a "zero print" or tiny volume at the extreme). Fade back into the range.
  3. **Stacked-imbalance continuation:** after a breakout (e.g., IB/ORB break), 3+ stacked buy imbalances form. Enter on the first pullback into the stacked zone; stop below it.
  4. **Delta divergence:** price makes a higher high while cumulative delta makes a lower high (or vice versa). Use it as a warning or as an entry trigger only at a key location.
  5. **DOM "refresh/pull" trades (Axia style):** a large resting bid/offer keeps refreshing after being hit (iceberg-like behaviour, so trade with it), or a large order is pulled as price approaches (possible spoof, so expect a break). Use mainly around news.
- Valentini's "Direction, Location, Aggression" is a clean way to sequence the methods in this file: AMT/profile for direction (balance vs imbalance), volume profile/VWAP/IB for location, footprint/CVD/tape for aggression (the trigger).
- **Platforms needed (from findings plus general domain knowledge):** footprint/delta requires tick-level bid/ask data from a futures feed (CQG, Rithmic, dxFeed, CME via broker). Commonly used: Sierra Chart, ATAS, NinjaTrader (Order Flow+), Jigsaw daytradr, Bookmap, Quantower, MotiveWave, Exocharts, DeepCharts, TradingView (limited footprint).
- Because spoofing in precious-metals futures was found at scale, resting size on the GC DOM should be treated as especially unreliable unless it trades.

## 5. Opening Range Breakout (Crabel), NR4/NR7/inside day, opening drives, gaps, first/last-hour intraday momentum: academic evidence

### Takeaway
Toby Crabel (1990) formalized the ORB with a volatility-based "stretch," plus contraction filters (NR4, NR7, inside day) to pick days likely to trend. Modern academic/quant work by Zarattini, Aziz and Barbon reports strong 5-minute ORB backtests on QQQ/TQQQ and especially on "Stocks in Play." An independent replication on index CFDs (incl. Nasdaq) found the **gross edge reproduces but the net edge is ~zero after realistic costs**. Peer-reviewed work (Gao-Han-Li-Zhou 2018 JFE; Baltussen-Da-Lammers-Martens 2021 JFE) robustly documents **market intraday momentum**: the first half-hour or rest-of-day return predicts the last half-hour, across 60+ futures, linked to gamma hedging. The effect is statistically real but small (R² ≈ 2% for SPY). Gap-fill statistics for ES/NQ are practitioner-sourced. Small gaps fill most of the time, mostly as arithmetic, and large gaps fill much less often.

### Cited Findings
**Crabel**
- Crabel, T. (1990). *Day Trading with Short Term Price Patterns and Opening Range Breakout.* Greenville: Traders Press. Goodreads lists first publication Aug 1, 1990, 288 pages. It covers opening range breakouts, short-term price patterns, expansion/contraction patterns, combinations, and opens/closes within price bars. — [Oxford Strat](https://oxfordstrat.com/?p=8238); [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/64536590-day-trading-with-short-term-price-patterns-and-opening-range-breakout)
- Crabel runs **Crabel Capital Management, LLC** (Milwaukee, WI). He worked for Victor Niederhoffer from 1991 until April 1993. — [Wikipedia: Toby Crabel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toby_Crabel)
- Crabel's ORB enters a predetermined distance (the **"stretch,"** determined by observation) above or below the open with buy-stop and sell-stop orders. One summary places the stops above/below the opening-range high/low by the stretch. His ORB series ran in *Technical Analysis of Stocks & Commodities* vol. 7 (1989), Parts 4–6. — [S&C Store: ORB Part 4](https://store.traders.com/-v07-c02-orb-pdf.html); [S&C Store: ORB Part 5](https://store.traders.com/-v07-c04-orb-pdf.html); [S&C Store: ORB Part 6](https://store.traders.com/-v07-c05-orb-pdf.html); [NexusFi: Opening Range Breakout](https://nexusfi.com/a/strategies/opening-range-breakout)
- **Inside day (ID)**: high and low both inside the prior day's range. Crabel's hypothesis was that IDs precede trend days and therefore successful ORBs. **NR4**: range narrower than each of the prior 3 days. **NR7**: narrower than each of the prior 6 days. Crabel found ORB trades the day after an **ID/NR4** tended to coincide with trend days, and secondary sources say the same for **NR7**. Secondary sources also caution that inside-bar breaks often fail. — [MarketInOut: Toby Crabel screens](https://www.marketinout.com/stock-screener/hub/toby-crabel); [Oxford Strat](https://oxfordstrat.com/?p=3707); [LuxAlgo: Inside Bar](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/inside-bar/); [Day Trading Toolkit: Inside Day Breakout](https://daytradingtoolkit.com/strategies/inside-day-breakout-strategy)

**Zarattini/Aziz/Barbon ORB papers**
- *"Can Day Trading Really Be Profitable?"* (Zarattini & Aziz, SSRN 4416622, Apr 10, 2023): 5-minute ORB on **QQQ, 2016–2023** (two bear markets). With leverage or leveraged ETFs, it reports significant returns vs buy-and-hold. **TQQQ-based ORB: +1,484% vs QQQ buy-and-hold +169%**, annualized alpha **33% net of commissions**. — [SSRN 4416622](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4416622); [Concretum Group](https://concretumgroup.com/tag/modelling/)
- Rule summary: if the first 5-minute bar is up (down), buy (sell) at the start of the second 5-minute bar. No trade if the first bar's open ≈ close (doji). — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/day-trading-with-an-opening-range-breakout-strategy)
- A replication blog reports the paper's **24% hit rate and +0.13 R per trade**, about 33%/year on QQQ with 4× leverage. It also notes the paper charges commission but **no spread or slippage** and has **no out-of-sample period**. Running the rule on index CFDs (**NQ/Nasdaq, SPX, Dow, DAX, FTSE**) for 2015–2026, **gross results matched the paper, but net of costs no market was distinguishable from zero and four of five were negative** (single independent blog). — [mql5 blog: ORB paper replicated on five indices](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)
- An author's MoneyShow deck concedes TQQQ buy-and-hold can beat day trading in bull markets (while leveraged products "get destroyed" in bear markets). It also flags possibly unrealistic results due to low assumed commission and slippage. — [MoneyShow deck (Bear Bull Traders PDF)](https://bearbulltraders.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/MoneyShow-Day-Trading-vs-Investing-ORB-.pdf)
- *"A Profitable Day Trading Strategy For The U.S. Equity Market"* (Zarattini, Barbon, Aziz; Feb 2024; SFI Research Paper 24-98): 5-min ORB on **7,000+ US stocks, 2016–2023**, restricted to **"Stocks in Play"** (abnormally high relative volume, usually on news). **Top-20 Stocks-in-Play portfolio: >1,600% total net, Sharpe 2.81, annualized alpha 36%**, vs S&P 500 passive +198%. Restricting to Stocks in Play was "a significant benefit, even after transaction costs." — [Univ. St. Gallen repository](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/handle/20.500.14171/122125); [IDEAS/RePEc rp2498](https://ideas.repec.org/p/chf/rpseri/rp2498.html)
- Filters (secondary summary): opening price > $5 and 14-day average volume ≥ 1,000,000 shares. — [CXO Advisory: ORB for overactive stocks](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/individual-investing/intraday-trading-of-overactive-stocks-via-opening-range-breakout/)
- QuantConnect replication (1,000 stocks instead of 7,000, otherwise the same parameters): Sharpe ≈ 2.4, beta near zero. — [QuantConnect: ORB for Stocks in Play](https://www.quantconnect.com/research/18444/opening-range-breakout-for-stocks-in-play/)

**Intraday momentum (peer-reviewed)**
- **Gao, Han, Li & Zhou, "Market Intraday Momentum," *Journal of Financial Economics* (2018):** the first half-hour return (measured from the prior close) predicts the **last half-hour** return. Data: high-frequency S&P 500 ETF, 1993–2013 (an earlier draft used SPY 1999–2012). The effect is stronger on volatile, high-volume, recession and **major macro-news days**, and also exists in ten other heavily traded ETFs. The proposed explanations are infrequent rebalancing (Bogousslavsky 2016) and late-informed trading near the close. — [WUSTL profile: Market intraday momentum](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/market-intraday-momentum/); [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/2014/08/attention-prop-traders-the-first-half-hour-of-trading-predicts-the-last-half-hour/)
- Predictive R² for SPY first-half-hour → last-half-hour is reported as **≈0.02 (2%)**. — [CXO Advisory: First and last half-hours linked](https://cxoadvisory.com/calendar-effects/first-and-last-half-hours-of-trading-linked)
- **Baltussen, Da, Lammers & Martens, "Hedging Demand and Market Intraday Momentum," *JFE* 142 (2021) 377–403:** studies **60+ futures** (equities, bonds, commodities, currencies), **1974–2020**, and finds intraday momentum in all of them. The final 30-minute return is predicted by the rest-of-day return, and it **reverses over following days**. The mechanism is **gamma hedging** by option market makers and leveraged-ETF rebalancing: short-gamma hedgers trade in the direction of the move. Momentum is much stronger on days of **negative net dealer gamma** and absent on positive-gamma days (per a secondary summary). — [Erasmus Univ. repository](https://pure.eur.nl/en/publications/hedging-demand-and-market-intraday-momentum/); [Alpha Architect: Does gamma hedging affect prices?](https://alphaarchitect.com/hot-topic-does-gamma-hedging-actually-affect-stock-prices/); [Authors' PDF (Notre Dame)](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)

**Gaps and gap fills (ES/NQ; practitioner)**
- **Scott Andrews ("the gap guy"; *Understanding Gaps*, Traders Press 2008):** a gap-zone map built on 12 years and ~3,000 gaps in S&P 500 futures. It sorts the open into **five zones** relative to the prior day's high, close, open and low, and uses a size cutoff of **40% of the 5-day ATR**: smaller gaps have a much higher fill probability and larger gaps much lower. A MoneyShow headline claims set-ups that work "almost 75% of the time" (unverified). — [MoneyShow transcript](https://moneyshow.com/articles/videotranstr-25181); [Podcast ep. 018: Scott Andrews the gap guy](https://sites.libsyn.com/66295/018-scott-andrews-the-gap-guy-talks-all-about-gaps-why-they-work-how-to-pick-the-best-gaps-and-avoid-the-worst-gap-zones-and-setting-stops-and-targets-plus-we-discuss-a-concept-called-ensemble-systems-that-may-just-change-the-way-you-view-trading-strategi)
- NexusFi (dataset unnamed, "5-year backtests" on 1-min data, unverified): **small gaps (0.25–0.5% of prior close) filled the same day 84% of the time, within the first hour 48%, average fill time 45 minutes.** Fill rate falls as gap size rises, and runaway/momentum gaps fill only ~50% of the time. — [NexusFi: Fill the Gap](https://nexusfi.com/a/concepts/fill-the-gap)
- SPY since 1993 (ETF, not futures): gaps of 0.1–0.25% filled the same day ≈78–80% of the time, while gaps >1% filled the same day only ≈26–33%. The source notes that high fill rates on small gaps are "mostly arithmetic, not an edge by themselves." — [thetrading.tools: Gap analysis](https://www.thetrading.tools/gap-analysis)
- A TradingView "ES/NQ Gap Fill Probability & Statistics" script claims ~2,600+ ES and ~2,700+ NQ days (2014–2024) classified by ATR-relative size, but doesn't publish the numbers. — [TradingView script](https://it.tradingview.com/script/32pGGSWx-Gap-Fill-Probability-Statistics-ES-NQ)
- A Nova SBE 2022 master's thesis builds a gap-fill strategy on 1-minute **E-mini S&P 500 cash-session** data from 01/03/2000 to 10/27/2021 (results not retrieved). — [Nova SBE thesis PDF](https://run.unl.pt/bitstream/10362/145336/1/individualcommon.pdf)

### Inferences
- **ORB catalogue for NQ/ES/MNQ/MES:**
  - *5-min ORB* (Zarattini-style): direction from the first 5-min RTH bar (9:30–9:35 ET). Enter at 9:35 ET; stop at the opposite side of the opening bar (inference about the paper's specifics; the replication reports "R" units); exit at a profit target or the close. The edge is thin per trade (+0.13R, 24% hit rate), so execution costs dominate. For futures, micros (MNQ/MES) cut position granularity, but commission per contract is proportionally higher.
  - *15/30/60-min ORB:* longer windows filter noise but give up more of the move. The 60-min ORB is the same as the Market Profile **IB breakout**.
  - *Crabel stretch ORB:* buy-stop at open + stretch, sell-stop at open − stretch, preferably on days after NR4/NR7/inside days.
- Combining the evidence: ORB/IB breakouts and intraday momentum are most likely to pay on **high-volatility, high-volume, macro-news and negative-gamma days**, and least likely in positive-gamma, low-volatility regimes. This links Sections 5 and 6.
- Last-half-hour momentum (rest-of-day return predicts 15:30–16:00 ET) is the best-documented intraday open/close effect. It is implementable in ES/NQ around the NYSE close and MOC imbalance releases.
- Gap-fill trading: "fade small gaps that open inside the prior range" is a plausible base rate, but the high fill rates partly reflect that small gaps need only small moves to fill. Large gaps beyond prior value with an open-drive are trend candidates, not fades.

### Gaps
- Crabel's exact stretch formula and his original win-rate tables were not retrieved. Commonly cited, not verified this session: stretch = 10-day average of the smaller of (high − open) and (open − low).
- No independent, cost-inclusive **ES/NQ futures** test of the 5/15/30/60-minute ORB with published results was found, apart from the single mql5 CFD replication.
- No peer-reviewed ES/NQ gap-fill statistics were found. The Nova thesis results weren't accessible.
- Exact stop/target rules and the 10R/EOD exit in "Can Day Trading Really Be Profitable?" could not be confirmed (full PDF inaccessible).

## 6. Futures-specific intraday mechanics: RTH vs ETH, overnight range, settlement, 8:30 ET releases, MOC, opex/GEX/0DTE, gold session behaviour

### Takeaway
ES/NQ trade nearly 24 hours on Globex (Sun 6 pm – Fri 5 pm ET, with a 5–6 pm ET daily halt). ES settles on a **30-second VWAP ending 3:00 pm CT (4:00 pm ET)** since October 2020. NYSE publishes the regulatory MOC imbalance at **3:50 pm ET**, which practitioners say moves ES/NQ into the close. **0DTE** options were ~59% of SPX volume in 2025 (≈63% by mid-2026, per SpotGamma), making dealer gamma a popular intraday regime filter. Academic evidence on 0DTE volatility is mixed (Dim-Eraker-Vilkov find a stabilizing or neutral effect). Gold trades ~23h, with key windows at the London open, the LBMA auctions (10:30 and 15:00 London) and the COMEX/NY open. Evidence on fix-time effects is thin.

### Cited Findings
**Sessions and settlement (CME)**
- E-mini S&P 500 settlement moved from **4:15 pm ET to 4:00 pm ET (3:00 pm CT)** in October 2020 for E-mini, S&P 500 and **Micro E-mini S&P 500** futures and options; trading hours did not change. — [CME FAQ: Daily Settlement Price Determination Time Change](https://cmegroup.com/education/articles-and-reports/faq-daily-settlement-price-determination-time-change.html)
- Settlement = VWAP of all Globex trades **14:59:30–15:00:00 CT** in the lead month, rounded to 0.25. The pre-2020 window was 15:14:30–15:15:00 CT. — [CME client wiki: E-mini / Micro E-mini S&P 500 settlement procedure](https://cmegroupclientsite.atlassian.net/wiki/x/E4DaBw); [Linn Software: settlement prices](https://www.linnsoft.com/support/settlement-prices-future-contracts)
- Globex hours: **Sunday 6:00 pm – Friday 5:00 pm ET**, with a daily maintenance break 5:00–6:00 pm ET. — [CME: E-mini S&P 500 contract specs](https://cmegroup.com/markets/equities/sp/e-mini-sandp500.contractSpecs.html)

**MOC / closing auction**
- NYSE: a **Regulatory Closing Imbalance is disseminated at 3:50 pm ET** (for imbalances of 500 round lots or more). After 3:50, only MOC/LOC orders that offset the imbalance are accepted. Informational imbalances update every ~1 second 3:50–3:58. Closing D-Orders are included from 3:55, and cancellations stop at 3:58. — [NYSE Auctions Closing Process Fact Sheet](https://www.nyse.com/publicdocs/nyse/NYSE_Auctions_Closing_Process_Fact_Sheet.pdf); [NYSE Quarterly Expiration Information Memo (Q1 2021)](https://www.nyse.com/publicdocs/nyse/markets/nyse/rule-interpretations/2021/Quarterly%20Expiration%20IM%20Q1%20-%2021%20-%20Final.pdf)
- Practitioner claim: the 3:50 imbalance can move ES/NQ, and late MOC, floor-broker and D-order flow can shift it until 3:58 (not independently confirmed). Traders compare S&P futures to the imbalance to see whether a move is market-wide or imbalance-driven. — [NexusFi: MOC Imbalance futures trading](https://nexusfi.com/a/market-structure/moc-imbalance-futures-trading); [S&C Q&A by Don Bright](https://store.traders.com/stcov274qaby3.html)

**Options gamma / 0DTE / GEX**
- 0DTE was **59% of SPX options volume in 2025** (Cboe data cited by SpotGamma) and is "approaching 63%" in mid-2026 per SpotGamma. — [SpotGamma: 0DTE Options Explained (June 2026)](https://spotgamma.com/0dte-options-explained/); [SpotGamma: Gamma Exposure](https://spotgamma.com/gamma-exposure)
- SpotGamma framework (vendor, proprietary): **positive dealer GEX stabilizes and negative GEX amplifies**, and it "does not predict direction." Key levels: **Gamma Flip** (net dealer gamma changes sign), **Call Wall, Put Wall, Volatility Trigger.** A self-reported claim that "68% of sessions close inside SpotGamma's GEX range" has no published methodology. — [SpotGamma: Gamma Exposure](https://spotgamma.com/gamma-exposure); [SpotGamma: 0DTE](https://spotgamma.com/0dte)
- **Dim, Eraker & Vilkov (2024), "0DTEs: Trading, Gamma Risk and Volatility Propagation"** (via secondary summaries): higher 0DTE open-interest gamma is associated with **lower** realized intraday volatility, volume effects are small and transient, and 0DTEs carry the largest volatility risk premium. The paper's conclusion is that 0DTEs don't destabilize the market. — [Harbourfront Quant: Rise of 0DTE options](https://harbourfrontquant.substack.com/p/the-rise-of-0dte-options-cause-for)
- A 2026 Princeton senior thesis finds the dealer delta-hedging channel adds little to intraday variance forecasts, but notes other work finds a positive association between 0DTE activity and volatility. A practitioner tick-level note argues open-interest gamma matters mostly in the morning and real-time gamma later in the day. — [Princeton thesis](https://theses-dissertations.princeton.edu/entities/publication/61115cd1-cb09-4a8b-9230-ff488fc2a386); [RTGamma Substack](https://rtgamma.substack.com/p/open-gamma-vs-real-time-gamma-which)
- Market intraday momentum across futures is much stronger on **negative net-gamma days** (Baltussen et al. 2021; see Section 5). — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/hot-topic-does-gamma-hedging-actually-affect-stock-prices/)

**Gold futures (GC/MGC) session behaviour**
- Session map (practitioner): Asian session ≈ 18:00–03:00 GMT, London opens ≈ 07:00 GMT, COMEX/New York activity from ≈ 13:30 GMT to 22:00 GMT. **LBMA gold auctions at 10:30 and 15:00 (London time; cited as GMT).** The London–NY overlap is widely described as the most liquid and volatile window (practitioner claim). — [FXStreet: Gold trading hours](https://fxstreet.com/education/guides/gold/what-time-does-the-gold-market-open-gold-trading-hours)
- Traditional COMEX "regular" (legacy pit) hours **8:20 am – 1:30 pm ET**; electronic Globex runs nearly 24h (sources give Sun 5/6 pm to Fri 4:15/5 pm ET, which is inconsistent). — [HW Online: Gold trading times](https://hw.online/faq/gold-trading-times-today/)
- Academic: a CBS (Copenhagen) thesis finds a "hat-shaped" intraday seasonality in gold, with prices rising during Asian/eastern hours and falling for the rest of the day, plus price drops around London fixing times in years of alleged manipulation (unreviewed thesis). — [CBS thesis PDF](https://research-api.cbs.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/59803241/651553_Thesis_Contract_13383.pdf)
- *Journal of Futures Markets* (2001), COMEX gold futures: intraday patterns strongly affect return volatility. **U.S. employment, GDP, CPI and personal-income** releases are the most market-moving announcements (old data). — [IDEAS/RePEc: J. Futures Markets v21 i3](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v21y2001i3p257-278.html); [EconBiz: What moves the gold market?](https://econbiz.de/Record/what-moves-the-gold-market-cai-jun/10011197558)

### Inferences
- **Reference levels an ES/NQ intraday trader marks pre-open:** overnight (ETH) high/low and its midpoint, prior RTH high/low/close, prior settlement, prior-day VAH/VAL/POC and naked POCs, weekly open, 8:30 ET data-release bar, IB (9:30–10:30), VWAP and AVWAPs, and dealer-gamma levels (gamma flip, call/put walls).
- **Overnight inventory logic:** if ETH is entirely above the prior RTH range/settlement (inventory net long), an early RTH push lower to "correct" inventory is common practitioner lore (not sourced here). The open relative to prior value and the ON range drives the open-type classification.
- **8:30 ET releases (CPI, NFP, PPI, retail sales):** these create the session's first volatility burst before the 9:30 RTH open. On those days the "true" opening range is arguably set by the 8:30 bar. Gao et al. find intraday momentum is stronger on macro-news days, and VWAP-reversion guides tell traders to skip event days.
- **Gamma regime as a strategy switch:** in positive-gamma regimes (price above the gamma flip), favour VWAP/VA reversion and IB fades. In negative-gamma regimes (below the flip), favour ORB/IB-breakout and late-day momentum. This is consistent with Baltussen et al. and Zarattini et al.'s gamma checks, but the specific level values are proprietary vendor estimates.
- **Gold:** traders typically treat the 8:20 ET COMEX open, the 8:30 ET U.S. data, and the London afternoon auction (15:00 London ≈ 10:00 ET) as the main intraday volatility windows. The Asian session is generally quieter, though the CBS thesis suggests a systematic Asian-hours drift.

### Gaps
- Not verified this session: the ES/NQ RTH definition (9:30–16:00 ET equity cash session, with the CME "RTH" futures convention often 9:30–16:15 ET), and exact NQ settlement and GC/MGC settlement times. Commonly stated: COMEX gold settlement ≈ 1:30 pm ET (12:29–12:30 CT window).
- Not verified this session: contract specs (ES $50/pt, MES $5/pt, NQ $20/pt, MNQ $2/pt, GC 100 oz, MGC 10 oz).
- No empirical study quantifying the 3:50 pm imbalance's effect on ES/NQ was found.
- No peer-reviewed study of opex/quad-witching intraday behaviour in ES/NQ was retrieved. Exact current LBMA auction timings were not confirmed from LBMA.
- No sourced statistics on ES/NQ behaviour around 8:30 ET releases (e.g., average range of the release bar) were found.

## 7. Intermarket and correlation tools: ES/NQ/YM/RTY relative strength, ZN, DXY, VIX

### Takeaway
Futures day traders use intermarket comparisons as **filters and confirmation, not standalone signals**. These include index divergences (one index failing to confirm a new high or low, the "SMT divergence" popularized in ICT circles), NQ leadership vs ES, bonds (ZN) for risk-on/risk-off, DXY for gold, and VIX for equity stress. Sourcing is mainly community/educational (NexusFi, TradingView). Correlations drift by year.

### Cited Findings
- **YM/ES is the tightest correlated pair, not NQ/ES** (contrary to common assumption). RTY/NQ is the weakest at **0.7482**, dropping to **0.6160 in 2024**. Correlations shift year to year. — [NexusFi: Intermarket correlation data](https://nexusfi.com/a/data/intermarket-correlation-data)
- When NQ separates from ES, YM and RTY in direction or magnitude, that signals either a sector rotation or a false breakout in progress. The core question is whether the divergence is meaningful or noise. — [NexusFi: Relative strength analysis for futures day trading](https://nexusfi.com/a/strategies/relative-strength-analysis-futures-day-trading)
- Risk-off: money flows into Treasuries (ZN/ZB up, yields down) while equities fall. A risk-off dollar surge often pushes **GC and CL lower**. VIX has a strong, consistent negative correlation with ES (described as typical, not current). — [NexusFi: Intermarket analysis](https://nexusfi.com/a/strategies/intermarket-analysis)
- Example divergence read: ES and YM strong while NQ and RTY lag (late 2025), framed as narrowing participation, plus MACD divergences on NQ/YM. — [TradingView idea: When Indexes Disagree](https://www.tradingview.com/chart/YM1!/JlMVl1oL-When-Indexes-Disagree-Evidence-Based-Clues-Heading-Into-2026/)
- SMT divergence scripts compare liquidity sweeps across indices (e.g., NQ makes a new low while ES does not). They are described as structure-mapping tools, not standalone signals. — [TradingView ICT/SMT scripts](https://vn.tradingview.com/scripts/ict/page-8/); [TradingView: Intermarket Divergence with Reliability Scoring](https://tw.tradingview.com/scripts/intermarket/)

### Inferences
- **Practical use for NQ/ES:** confirm an ORB/IB breakout only if the sister index (ES for NQ, NQ for ES) also breaks its own reference level. Treat a non-confirmation (SMT divergence) at a prior high/low as a fade candidate. Use ZN direction and VIX/VX futures as risk-regime context, and use RTY for breadth.
- **For gold (GC/MGC):** watch DXY (or 6E inverted) and real yields (ZN/TIPS proxies) as primary intermarket drivers. A strong dollar on risk-off is typically gold-negative intraday per the source above, though gold's safe-haven role can override this.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed evidence was found that intraday index-divergence (SMT) signals improve ES/NQ strategy expectancy.

## 8. Forex: why order-flow tools are limited in spot FX and how traders work around it (6E, CME FX futures, tick volume)

### Takeaway
Spot FX is decentralized OTC: no central order book, no consolidated volume, and broker-specific feeds. Retail "volume" on MT4/MT5 is **tick volume** (count of price changes), which cannot show aggressor side, block size, icebergs or passive absorption. The standard workaround is to read **CME FX futures** (6E for EUR/USD, etc.), which have a full exchange order book and real traded volume, and execute in spot. The caveats: CME FX futures are a secondary venue relative to the huge spot market, and evidence on how well tick volume or futures volume proxies spot is contested.

### Cited Findings
- Forex is decentralized. Each broker is its own market, with no central book consolidating total volume per pair. Brokers use different liquidity-provider combinations, so tick volume differs across brokers. — [EarnForex: Tick volume in Forex](https://www.earnforex.com/guides/tick-volume-in-forex); [TradingWyckoff: Tick volume vs real volume](https://tradingwyckoff.com/en/tick-volume-vs-real-volume/)
- A bank's large block and a batch of small retail orders can each register as a single tick. Tick volume "cannot differentiate between the two," and cannot reveal directional aggressive order flow, hidden iceberg orders or passive limit absorption. — [FXNX: What tick volume cannot tell you](https://fxnx.com/en/blog/balance-volume-forex-what-tick-volume-cannot-tell-you)
- The "most accurate proxy for Spot FX" is CME currency futures such as **6E (EUR/USD)**, which trade on an exchange with a full order book and real data. The usual method is to analyze futures and execute in spot, given the high price correlation. — [FXNX: Mastering Forex Order Flow](https://fxnx.com/en/blog/mastering-forex-order-flow-tools-setup-guide); [TradingWyckoff](https://tradingwyckoff.com/en/tick-volume-vs-real-volume/)
- Contested: one source says tick volume "seems to be mostly uncorrelated" with real futures volume, though it expects some correlation. Another notes that cumulative delta on CME FX contracts reflects a **secondary market** tracking the much larger spot market. — [EarnForex: Do you believe in Forex tick volume?](https://www.earnforex.com/blog/do-you-believe-in-forex-tick-volume/); [ATAS Learn: Where it works](https://learn.atas.net/volume-basics/volume-analysis/where-it-works)
- Limits of MT5 tick data (what it can and cannot reveal). — [mql5 blog: Tick Data on MT5](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777201)
- **CME FX scale (2025):** FX futures and options average daily notional ≈ **$87B** (Feb 2025). In Dec 2025, ≈ **$93B**/day notional, down 4.7% YoY (contracts −10%). FX futures **open interest hit a record 3.1M contracts on Dec 11, 2025**. One summary lists FX ≈ 980K contracts ADV for 2025. CME's EBS spot platform averaged ≈ **$64B/day** (spot + NDF) in June 2025, up 11.1% vs 2024. CME's all-product 2025 ADV was a record **28.1M contracts**. — [The Full FX: CME FX volumes](https://thefullfx.com/cme-highlights-options-new-fx-spot-peak-as-fx-volumes-drift/); [The Full FX: Mixed FX volumes at CME](https://thefullfx.com/?p=6055); [Markets Media: CME record quarterly and June ADV](https://www.marketsmedia.com/cme-sets-record-quarterly-and-june-adv); [Metalsmine/CME: record annual ADV 28.1M](https://www.metalsmine.com/news/1377772-cme-group-reports-record-annual-adv-of-281/amp); [Bitcoinist: CME 2025 records](https://bitcoinist.com/cme-group-hits-all-time-highs-crypto-and-rates-fuel-record-2025-trading/amp/)

### Inferences
- **FX workarounds, ranked by data quality:**
  1. Use **CME FX futures** (6E, 6B, 6J, 6A, 6C, 6S; micros M6E etc.) for footprint, delta, volume profile and DOM, and map levels to spot. Mind the futures-spot basis (interest-rate differential), the quarterly roll, and inverted quotes for some pairs (6J and 6C quote USD/JPY and USD/CAD inversely).
  2. Use **EBS/primary-venue or aggregated ECN data** if available (institutional).
  3. Use **broker tick volume** only as a rough activity proxy (session timing, relative spikes), not for delta or absorption. Use **VWAP/volume profile on tick volume** cautiously.
- Session/time-based methods (London open range, NY open, ORB on the London session) and **intraday momentum** (Baltussen et al. found it in currency futures too) transfer to FX without needing true volume.
- For gold, the same logic applies: XAU/USD spot CFD traders lack real volume, so GC/MGC futures order flow is the proxy.

- No peer-reviewed study measuring the 6E-to-EUR/USD spot order-flow relationship or the tick-volume/real-volume correlation was found.
- Prop-firm (Topstep, Apex) educational content on order flow was not retrieved beyond Topstep's ES-indicator blog.
